# `outrun-producer@2` software model and selected delivery

The source candidate selects the corrected request model with `[FFB] Model = outrun-producer@2`, or the explicit F6 **Force model at the next start** choice. F6 saves only the selected model identifier; **Wheel output at the next start** independently saves `Output = wheel | none`, default `none`. Neither edit applies live. Producer@2 can request wheel delivery through the existing mute, process, foreground, device, ownership, F6/F8 and shutdown gates. Its software evidence does **not** qualify physical force, wheel polarity or feel.

Absent Model or `outrun-legacy@1` keeps the historical calculation as a **software trace-only reference**. Even a saved `Output = wheel` refuses before `WheelFfb_Load` or `Delivery::start`, with a message to select producer@2. `OR2_FFB_TRACE` and latched runs retain the legacy adapter, math, old tune keys, trace schema and goldens. Those goldens reproduce the old Tweaks calculation with synthetic inputs; they do **not** establish tire forces in this game's car record. The owner's installed tune is not migrated or reinterpreted. This source change is not evidence that the installed executable has been updated.

## Pin and fixed preview profile

`windows/vendor/native/forcemodel/force_model.h` is byte-for-byte toolkit `native/forcemodel/force_model.h` at `fb710dffa29f707d296f1f579851e23d549a8b51`, SHA-256 `C7F3C3FF9C6D1475B88015643AFB1B515D3F6F97BCC46E1FE2082F26DED1B16F`. The adapter uses its `Model`, `Inputs` availability flags and `Shaper`. The provisional software profile fixes spring strength at `0.30`, full spring speed at `30 m/s`, and symmetric shift strength at `0.05`; damping, tyre load, texture, impact and weight transfer are off. Shaper gain is unity, with no extra fade, smoothing or ramp in this offline preview. None of these constants comes from or changes the owner's tune.

## Sample contract

The desktop `input_filter` runs at `xbox/source/main.cpp:246`, before the next native race update. Its car bytes are therefore the **previous completed car tick**. `race_input.cpp:73-96` computes signed steering in `analog_7d6810.current[0]` (-127..127); `race_player_car.cpp:375` passes that consumed value to `operation_input_49fad0`, and `pc_common_control.cpp:2189-98` writes it shifted left by 8 at car `+0x202` in GAME. Positive game steering produces a negative centering request through the toolkit model, whose positive force pushes toward positive steer. The steer has the sign of the steering device's DirectInput X axis, which increases to the right, and `ffb_live.cpp` maps the model force to `SetDeviceForcesXY`'s x once, by the device's product: `ffbdev::wheel_direction` (`ffb_device.hpp`). See [Wheel direction](#wheel-direction).

`pc_common_control.cpp:1039-46` produces `+0x1F8` for the HUD; `race_hud_navi.cpp:691-704` displays that metric base unchanged or multiplied by `0.6215` for miles. The request uses `speed_mps = hud_kmh_1f8 / 3.6`. `pc_transmission.cpp:6-35` updates gear `+0x208`. A change between two valid, nonzero gears in one continuous car generation emits one `gear_shift` event. It does not fire on the first sample, a menu return, a new race or a respawn.

The pure `Frame` requires an external, nonzero race epoch and car generation, a strictly consecutive source tick **within one epoch and car generation**, positive bounded `dt`, and proof that `OperationInput` ran for this car on that source tick. The source counter may restart when identity changes. The bytes alone cannot establish that proof. The game's `+0xD50 > 120` branch skips the `+0x202` write, so the adapter independently rejects that stale-steering case. A menu/pause/goal frame, invalid input, source-tick gap, new epoch or changed car generation resets gear history, model state and shaped output. A newly entered epoch or generation primes with zero output. The optional desktop observer witnesses successful `0x4A6ED0` init, direct GamePlCar control and the exact `0x49FAD0` steering write. The shadow separately invalidates successful in-place tow/snap mutations; the ordinary witness has not observed a recovery, so physical integration remains gated on runtime qualification.

Only `Inputs.has_steer`, `steer`, `speed_mps` and one-tick `gear_shift` can be populated. `has_steer_rate`, `has_front_lateral_force`, `has_lateral_g`, `has_longitudinal_g`, `has_slip` and `has_drift` stay false. Texture and impact stay zero. Car `+0x1D0/+0x1D4` are speed differences, and `+0x264/+0x268` are road-edge cross-distances, so they never enter these request fields. `+0x24C..+0x258` surface words can be stale when road result 1 skips their write, so they remain unavailable too.

`Row` also carries an observation-only wall classification; nothing reads it into the model, so impact stays zero. It reads the wall-rebound timer `+0x283`, contact side `+0x281` and wall-contact bit `+0x4 & 0x20000` (`car_body_wall_coli_check`). `+0x283` has one nonzero writer, `cw_rebound_status` setting 30 (`pc_wall_rebound.cpp:164`, original `0x503BC3`), counts down at `pc_common_control.cpp:689`, and is zeroed by car init at `0x4A7917`. A computed row reports `Rebound` when `+0x283` strictly rises against the previous computed or primed sample, and `Scrape` for wall contact without a rise. Priming takes the current timer as its baseline, so a timer still running at start, respawn, resume or after a reset is not a new rebound. The hit-reaction counter `+0x282` is excluded because a scrape re-arms it every contact tick; traffic hits are not covered. The trace schema is unchanged.

The [player-model boundary](FFB-PRODUCER2-PLAYER-BOUNDARY.md) traces the recovery call sites and independent settings/selection work. The ordinary software witness does not qualify a physical output path.

`trace_header()` and `trace_csv()` expose the versioned `dbce.outrun-producer-ffb-trace@2` software row, including the consumed `dt_seconds` so event evolution can be replayed. This is a separate trace schema; it neither rewrites the legacy trace nor writes a runtime file. The fixture `test_ffb_producer2` exercises the actual `pc_input_analog_453860`, `operation_input_49fad0` and `manual_transmission` producers, previous-tick sampling, sign, shift count, unavailable fields and neutral/reset gates. Its synthetic car record verifies source behavior; it is not a rendered/gameplay or physical wheel qualification.

## Optional just-completed runtime shadow

Set `OR2_FFB_PRODUCER2_SHADOW_TRACE` to an absolute path before starting an **ordinary, non-recording** desktop process. The file is created only if absent. Without the switch, an unselected producer has no observer registration, model instance or file; selecting producer@2 registers the shared observer independently of optional tracing. Record requests refuse the switch; latched tests and Playback clear it before configuration. The F6 panel remains outermost in the input chain; it does not start recording. This path may coexist with the legacy `OR2_FFB_TRACE` software trace, with its unchanged legacy schema; Legacy wheel delivery is refused as described above.

The native observer runs after direct GamePlCar control and reads signed consumed steer `+0x202`, HUD speed `+0x1F8` and gear `+0x208` from that just-completed control body. It requires a successful car init, mode 16, a witnessed same-call `0x49FAD0` write, signed `+0xD50 <= 120`, no game fault, and no PasPlCar nesting. Its source tick is a per-generation control-attempt ordinal, including rejected attempts. F6 open and `event_state.pause_depth > 0` reset the shadow even when mode stays 16 or no car callback runs. A new race epoch or car generation primes silently. A repeated `(frame_counter_95af0c, update_index_8a8cdc)` native tick pair resets/refuses the duplicate. Successful in-place player tow/snap calls increment a separate continuity token; delayed tow holds the shadow neutral through the countdown, its snap records another zero row, and the next proven direct sample primes with cleared model, shaper and gear history. A traffic-car crash tow that mutates shared player body/wheel work also invalidates player continuity. `car_generation` remains the successful `0x4A6ED0` init count.

The shadow CSV is `dbce.outrun-producer-ffb-shadow-trace@3`. Rows add an `origin` (`frame` for input-filter neutral rows, `control` for completed car calls), the native tick pair, gate reason, `continuity_token` and `recovery_pending` before the pure @2 columns. This versions the added fields without changing the pure `dbce.outrun-producer-ffb-trace@2` schema. The shadow's header says `seam=just-completed-car-control`; the pure adapter's `previous-completed-car-tick` header describes its original input-filter fixture and is not used here. An @1 legacy row samples **before** an update, whereas the shadow row samples **after** completion. Frame numbers alone therefore do not align their measurements exactly. The optional shadow trace does not itself send a wheel command. With producer@2 selected, a separate request sink shares the observer and feeds the existing gated delivery path. Observer exceptions and file write failures detach/reset only this optional trace, leaving game success, input, the legacy reference and any selected request sink unaffected. Rows use buffered OS writes on the game thread and flush once at detach/shutdown; a crash may lose the trace tail. `test_ffb_shadow` exercises the gates and injected failures with a fake writer; no game, device, or physical force is involved.

## Wheel direction

Which x sign centres is a per-wheel fact (toolkit `knowledge/DIRECTINPUT-FFB.md`), so only a measured product is driven. The **MOZA R12 Base** (`{0006346e-0000-0000-0000-504944564944}`) is direction **-1**: a positive x turns it left. That comes from two earlier measurements on this rig. art-of-sim-rally, 2026-09-02, used this library's encoding: `+Fy` centres, and a left turn pushes the wheel right. Its `Fy` has the steer's sign. cruisn-collection, 2026-08, found that a positive SDL level turns the R12 left. Before this mapping, the first delivered producer@2 drive (owner, 2026-10-18, exe `00491F11`) sent the centring force with the model's sign. On the R12 that is anti-centring, and the owner felt a weak force that seemed to push outward. Any other product, an empty one or a malformed one is released at the first race sample in front, before any device call or shim hand-over. The status then reads "this steering wheel's force direction is not measured (product ...), so no force is sent; the controls are unaffected". The product comes from the native layer's DirectInput listing of the steering instance. On the shim backend it comes from the steer binding, which Core checks against the opened handle, and the hand-over starts only from a live sample of it. The corrected direction is **not yet verified at the wheel**. The check: in a race at speed, turn and release, and the wheel should return toward centre. Strength defaults are unchanged. At 50%, a 20% steer at 100 km/h is about 5.6% of full scale before the base's own gain.
